Short answer first: **Winter Fuel Payment is £100 to £300 for people born on or before 27 June 1960, and most get it automatically. Cold Weather Payment is £25 for each 7-day spell of very cold weather, and only some people on certain benefits qualify.**

They are two different payments with different rules, and people mix them up. Everything below comes from GOV.UK pages I read on 30 October 2026: [Winter Fuel Payment](https://www.gov.uk/winter-fuel-payment) and [Cold Weather Payment](https://www.gov.uk/cold-weather-payment). Rules and amounts change, so check those pages before you rely on anything here.

## Winter Fuel Payment: who gets it

For winter 2026 to 2027, GOV.UK says you can get it if you were born on or before 27 June 1960 and usually live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. The qualifying week is 21 to 27 September 2026, and your amount depends on your circumstances in that week.

You won't get it if, for example, you were in hospital getting free treatment, or in prison, for the whole of that week, or if your leave to enter the UK says you can't claim public funds. The eligibility page also has special rules for care homes.

If you live in Scotland, GOV.UK says you cannot get it. You might be eligible for the Pension Age Winter Heating payment instead, which is a separate Scottish payment.

## How much Winter Fuel Payment is it?

GOV.UK lists these amounts. The simplest cases:

| Your situation | Born 28 Sep 1946 to 27 Jun 1960 | Born before 28 Sep 1946 |
|---|---|---|
| Living alone, or nobody you live with is eligible | £200 | £300 |
| Living in a care home (if eligible) | £100 | £150 |

If you live with another eligible person and neither of you gets Pension Credit, Universal Credit or income-related ESA, the amounts are lower: £100 each if you were both born between those dates, and £150 each if you were both born before 28 September 1946. Mixed-age couples get £100 or £200 depending on who is older. Where you or your partner do get one of those benefits, GOV.UK has separate rules for joint claims. Use [the amounts page](https://www.gov.uk/winter-fuel-payment/how-much-youll-get) for your exact household.

## How and when it's paid

Most people don't need to claim. GOV.UK says that if you are eligible you will get a letter in October or November telling you how much you will get, and most eligible people will be paid in November or December 2026.

The payment doesn't affect your other benefits. It does need watching if your income is over £35,000. Then HMRC takes the payment back, usually through your tax code, or through your tax bill if you complete Self Assessment. Your partner's income doesn't count towards the total. GOV.UK says you can opt out if your income is over £35,000 or you simply don't want the payment, and the [recovery page](https://www.gov.uk/winter-fuel-payment/if-your-income-is-over-35000) links to the steps.

One more warning from GOV.UK: be wary of unsolicited calls, emails or messages asking for bank details or passwords. The payment is automatic, so nobody legitimate needs to ask you for them.

## Cold Weather Payment: who gets it

This one is weather-triggered and benefit-linked. GOV.UK says you get £25 for each 7-day period of very cold weather between 1 November 2026 and 31 March 2027. A payment is made when the average temperature in your area is recorded as, or forecast to be, at or below zero degrees Celsius over 7 consecutive days.

You may qualify if you get certain benefits, and GOV.UK adds a condition for each:

- **Pension Credit:** you will usually get it.
- **Income-related Employment and Support Allowance:** usually if you are in the work-related activity group or the support group. Other routes exist, such as a pensioner premium or a child under 5 living with you.
- **Universal Credit:** usually if neither you nor your partner is employed or gainfully self-employed, and you have a health condition or disability that limits your capability for work, or a child under 5 lives with you.
- **Support for Mortgage Interest:** usually if you are treated as getting a qualifying benefit, with similar extra conditions.

The [eligibility page](https://www.gov.uk/cold-weather-payment/eligibility) has the full wording, and it is worth reading properly because the conditions are specific.

## How Cold Weather Payments are triggered and paid

You don't apply. GOV.UK says you should be paid within 14 working days after each period of very cold weather in your area, into the same account as your benefit payments. You may need to report a change if you have a baby or a child under 5 comes to live with you.

To see whether your area has triggered one, England and Wales have a postcode checker at coldweatherpayments.dwp.gov.uk, and Northern Ireland has one on nidirect.gov.uk. People in Scotland can't get Cold Weather Payments but may get an annual Winter Heating Payment that doesn't depend on local weather.

## Other help worth knowing about

GOV.UK's Winter Fuel Payment page also points to the Warm Home Discount, described there as a £150 discount on bills if you get Pension Credit or live in a low-income household, and to cost-of-living help from your local council, which depends on local rules.

If you or someone you look after needs extra support when the power is off, the free [Priority Services Register](/blog/priority-services-register-who-can-join/) is worth a phone call too.
