Is lagging your pipes and hot water cylinder worth it? **Yes for the cylinder, if it has no jacket or only a thin one, and it is a small but cheap win for exposed hot water pipes.** The Energy Saving Trust says an existing cylinder jacket should be at least 80mm thick, and puts pipe insulation at around £6 a year.

Neither job will change your life. Both are cheap, need no tradesperson, and keep paying back every winter. Here is what the evidence says, the sums I can stand behind, and where I would start.

## What the Energy Saving Trust says

The Energy Saving Trust's page on [insulating tanks, pipes and radiators](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/insulating-tanks-pipes-and-radiators/) gives these figures for Great Britain:

| Job | Figure given |
| --- | --- |
| Cylinder jacket, cost | about £30 |
| Cylinder jacket, thickness | at least 80mm for an existing jacket |
| Insulating exposed hot water pipes | around £6 a year saved |

They are the current numbers as far as I can tell, but check the page before you spend money, as the Trust updates them.

## Why a cylinder loses heat

If you have a hot water cylinder, the water in it is heated by your boiler or an immersion heater and then waits to be used. Any heat that leaks through the sides is paid for twice: once to put it in, and again when the water has to be reheated.

Many cylinders have factory foam, and some have a loose fabric jacket on top. A thin or tired jacket does little. The Trust's advice is that an existing jacket should be at least 80mm thick, and if yours is thinner, it is worth replacing.

I have not found a current, sourced saving in pounds for topping up a cylinder, so I am not going to invent one. Older press pieces quote bigger numbers for fitting a jacket to a bare cylinder, but I could not tie them to a current source, so I have left them out.

## What does the heat actually cost you?

You can see what is at stake without any guesswork by pricing the energy. These are the Flexible Octopus standard rates for London (region C), including VAT, from the Octopus API on 2 November 2026:

| Fuel | Unit rate |
| --- | --- |
| Electricity | 26.6p per kWh |
| Gas | 8.15p per kWh |

An immersion heater is the simple case. The typical figure on the [Cheap Watts](/) site is a 3kW element running for an hour, which is 3 kWh. At 26.6p that is about 80p for every hour it heats. Every kilowatt hour that leaks out of the cylinder is 26.6p you pay to replace.

Now the pipe saving. If the Trust's £6 a year is gas, it is about 74 kWh (£6 divided by 8.15p). If it were electric heating, it would be about 23 kWh. Small, which is why I call pipes a tidy-up and not a project.

## Where to lag first

Not every pipe is worth the effort. I would work in this order:

1. **The cylinder.** Check what is on it. If there is no jacket, or it is thin or squashed, replace it with an 80mm jacket from a DIY shop. It is the biggest surface area.
2. **Hot water pipes within a metre or so of the cylinder.** These stay warm and lose heat the most, and they are the easiest to reach.
3. **Any other exposed hot water pipes** in cold spaces such as lofts, garages and under floors.
4. **Cold pipes in unheated areas.** This is about freezing risk, not bills. If you want advice on that, read your home insurer's guidance or [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/).

Before you touch any hot water pipe, turn off the heating and hot water and let the pipes cool. Hot pipes can burn you, and the job is easier on cold pipes anyway.

Pipe lagging is usually foam tubing cut to length and taped at the joints. I have not priced it, because the cost varies by shop and pipe size. Measure the pipe run first, then compare.

## What it will not do

Lagging cuts waste. It does not cut how much hot water you use. If you want a bigger saving on the same boiler, the free setting is usually the flow temperature, which I covered in [my post on turning your boiler down to 60°C](/blog/boiler-flow-temperature-60-degrees-cut-gas-bill/). Heat leaking from the home is a different job again, and cheap draught-proofing is covered in [my draught-proofing post](/blog/draught-proofing-on-a-budget-uk/).

Combi boilers heat water on demand and have no cylinder, so the jacket advice does not apply to you. Pipe lagging still can if you have long, exposed runs of hot pipe.

## Is it worth it?

- **Cylinder with no jacket or a thin one:** worth doing. It costs about £30 per the Trust and the heat you stop leaking is paid-for energy.
- **Cylinder already fitted with an 80mm jacket:** nothing to do.
- **Exposed hot pipes:** worth a weekend if you already have the foam. Around £6 a year is a modest saving.
- **Combi boiler, no cylinder:** skip the jacket and look at pipes only.
