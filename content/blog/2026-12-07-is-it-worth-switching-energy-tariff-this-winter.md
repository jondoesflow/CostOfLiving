Short answer: **for a lot of households on a standard variable tariff, yes, it's worth checking now. On 28 November 2026 the cheapest 12 month fixes I found for Yorkshire were around 27p per kWh of electricity, against a capped rate of about 32p from 1 January. Whether a fix actually pays depends on how much you use and what the cap does after March, and you can work that out with your own numbers.**

The price cap goes up on 1 January 2027. Ofgem's [unit rates page](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/your-energy-supply/your-energy-bill/energy-price-cap-unit-rates-and-standing-charges) puts the average electricity rate at 32.69p per kWh from January, up from 26.32p, and gas at 10.96p, up from 7.97p. Standing charges barely move.

So the real question this winter is fixed or variable. Here's how to answer it for your own home.

## What's on offer right now

I took these from the Octopus, EDF and E.ON Next public price data on 28 November 2026. They're electricity prices for Yorkshire, paying by monthly Direct Debit. Your region will differ a little, so the gaps between them are the useful part.

| Tariff | Unit rate | Standing charge | Exit fee per fuel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard variable today (Flexible Octopus, EDF, E.ON Next) | 25.60p per kWh | 60.89p to 61.67p a day | None |
| E.ON Next Flex from 1 January 2027 | 32.11p per kWh | 61.59p a day | None |
| Octopus 12M Fixed | 27.87p per kWh | 60.89p a day | £50 |
| Octopus 18M Fixed | 26.68p per kWh | 60.89p a day | £75 |

E.ON Next's standard variable tariff already shows its January price, which is a good guide to the capped rate in Yorkshire from 1 January: 32.11p per kWh.

A few things jump out:

- **Every fix here is dearer than the variable rate in December.** The variable rate is still on the October cap until 31 December.
- **Most of them are cheaper than the variable rate from January.** The 12 and 18 month fixes are about 4p to 5.5p per kWh below it.
- **Standing charges on the fixes are almost the same as the variable tariff.** So the unit rate does nearly all the work, apart from the Lower Daily one.
- **Longer isn't always cheaper.** Octopus's 18 month fix is below its 12 month one, but E.ON and EDF's two year deals are dearer than E.ON's 12 month fix.

The E.ON 12 month and Lower Daily fixes say you'll need to manage your account online. Check the supplier's own page for any conditions like that before you pick one.

## The sum that decides it

A fix beats the variable tariff only if the variable rate, averaged over the whole length of your fix, ends up higher than the fixed rate. You only know part of that.

What we know: the variable rate is 25.60p in December and 32.11p from January to March in Yorkshire. What we don't know: anything after 31 March. Ofgem [sets the cap every three months](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/your-energy-supply/your-energy-bill/energy-price-cap-and-standing-charges-explained), so April's rate hasn't been announced yet.

Here's how I'd think about it. Say you fix from 1 January on E.ON's 12 month deal at 26.89p:

1. **January to March, you're ahead.** Every kWh costs 5.22p less than it would on the variable rate (32.11p minus 26.89p).
2. **From April, you've got a lead to defend.** Each month the cap sits below 26.89p chips away at it. Each month it stays above 26.89p adds to it.

## A worked example

These are my assumptions, not your figures. I've used [Ofgem's typical household](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/summary-of-changes-to-energy-price-cap-1-January-to-31-March-2027.pdf) of 2,500 kWh of electricity a year, and assumed it's spread evenly, so 625 kWh falls in January to March and 1,875 kWh in the rest of the year. If your home uses more in winter than that, the fix looks better.

- **January to March saving:** 625 kWh × 5.22p = £32.63.
- **Break-even for the rest of the year:** £32.63 spread across 1,875 kWh is 1.74p per kWh. So the variable rate would have to average below 26.89p minus 1.74p, which is about 25.15p, from April to December for the variable tariff to win.

The variable rate today, before the January rise, is 25.60p. So on these numbers the fix comes out ahead unless the cap averages below about 25.15p from April to December, a bit under today's level. Nobody knows whether it will, me included.

The standing charges make almost no difference here. E.ON's fix is 61.67p a day against 61.59p on the variable rate from January, which is about 30p over a year.

## Check it with your own data

Your own answer comes from three numbers you can find yourself.

1. **Your yearly electricity use in kWh.** It's on your annual statement or in your supplier's app. If you have a smart meter, you can download your half-hourly use; I explained how in [how to read your smart meter and download your usage](/blog/read-smart-meter-download-half-hourly-usage/).
2. **What you used last January to March.** Look at the monthly view in your app or your bills for those months. This is the bit an average can't tell you, and it's where a fix earns its money.
3. **Your current tariff and end date.** If you're already on a fix, nothing changes until it ends.

Then multiply your January to March kWh by the gap between the fixed rate and 32.11p (or your own region's January rate). That's your head start. Divide it by the kWh you use the rest of the year, and you've got how far below the fixed rate the cap would need to average from April.

## Low user? Look at the standing charge

The E.ON Lower Daily Fixed 24m deal turns the usual trade around: a higher unit rate, a lower standing charge. Against E.ON's ordinary 24 month fix it saves 16.97p a day on the standing charge, about £61.94 a year, and costs 3.43p more per kWh.

£61.94 divided by 3.43p is about 1,806 kWh. So if you use less than roughly 1,800 kWh of electricity a year, the lower standing charge version works out cheaper of the two. Above that, the ordinary one does. This is where your own yearly figure matters.

## What about gas?

The same logic applies, and exit fees are charged per fuel, so a dual fuel fix with a £50 fee costs £100 to leave early. On 28 November 2026 in Yorkshire, E.ON's Next Flex showed gas at 10.89p per kWh from 1 January, up from 7.89p now. Octopus's 12M Fixed had gas at 9.32p and E.ON's 12 month fix had 9.42p, both with standing charges a little under the 29.73p a day E.ON's variable rate shows from January.

## If you're on a fix already

Check the end date first. [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/energy/energy-supply/get-a-better-energy-deal/switching-energy-supplier/) says you can switch once you have 49 days or less left on a fixed contract. With 50 days or more left, you might have to pay an exit fee.

If your fix ends before 31 March, you'd normally roll onto your supplier's variable tariff, at the January rate from 1 January. That's the group with the most reason to look now.

## The short version

- From 1 January, E.ON Next's variable rate in Yorkshire is 32.11p per kWh. Several fixes on 28 November 2026 were about 4p to 5.5p below that.
- Fixes cost a little more in December, and nobody knows the cap after March.
- On average usage, E.ON's 12 month fix at 26.89p wins unless the cap averages below about 25.15p from April.
- Low users should compare standing charges too, because a lower daily charge can beat a lower unit rate.
- These were the rates on 28 November 2026. Check the supplier's site on the day you switch.

Next step: open your supplier's app, find what you used last January to March, and multiply it by 5p. That's roughly what a fix could save you in the three months we actually know about.
