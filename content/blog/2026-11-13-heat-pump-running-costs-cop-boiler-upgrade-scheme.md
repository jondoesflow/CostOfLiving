Is a heat pump cheaper to run than a gas boiler? It depends on one number: the COP, or coefficient of performance. On Flexible Octopus prices in London on 4 November 2026, a heat pump needs a COP of about 2.9 to match a 90% efficient gas boiler on running cost. Above that it is cheaper, below it dearer. On a time-of-use tariff such as Cosy Octopus, the bar is much lower.

Here is how that works, and what the government grant covers.

## What COP means

A gas boiler turns each unit of gas into slightly less than one unit of heat. A heat pump works differently: it moves heat from the outside air (or the ground) into your home. That means one unit of electricity can deliver more than one unit of heat.

COP is heat out divided by electricity in. A COP of 3 means 1 kWh of electricity gives 3 kWh of heat. The figure changes with the weather and with how hot your radiators need to run. A better guide than a headline COP is the seasonal figure over a whole year (often called SCOP or SPF), so ask your installer for it in writing and for what it is based on.

## The break-even sum

Electricity costs more than gas per unit, so the heat pump has to make up the difference with efficiency.

On 4 November 2026, the Flexible Octopus rates in London, including VAT, from the [Octopus Energy API](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/VAR-22-11-01/), were:

- Electricity: 26.60p per kWh (VAT on household electricity is 0% at present, as covered in [the Budget post](/blog/autumn-budget-2026-energy-bills-what-to-expect/)).
- Gas: 8.15p per kWh.

I have assumed a gas boiler that is 90% efficient. That is my assumption, so swap in your own boiler's figure. Each kWh of heat from gas then costs 8.15 ÷ 0.9 = 9.05p.

The heat pump matches that when 26.60 ÷ COP = 9.05, so COP = 2.94. With an older 80% boiler (10.18p per kWh of heat), the break-even is 2.61.

Per 1,000 kWh of heat delivered, at those prices:

| Heating | Cost |
|---|---|
| Gas boiler, 90% efficient | £90.51 |
| Heat pump, COP 2 | £132.98 |
| Heat pump, COP 3 | £88.65 |
| Heat pump, COP 4 | £66.49 |

These are my sums from the rates above, not measurements from a real home. The point is the shape: the same heat pump can cost more or less than gas depending on how well it performs in your house.

## Time-of-use tariffs change the sum

A heat pump can shift when it heats. On 4 November 2026, Cosy Octopus in London had three rates ([Octopus Energy API](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/COSY-22-12-08/)):

- 14.12p per kWh from 4am to 7am, 1pm to 4pm and 10pm to midnight.
- 28.80p per kWh for the rest of the day, apart from the peak.
- 43.19p per kWh at the peak, from 4pm to 7pm.

At 14.12p, a COP of 3 delivers 1,000 kWh of heat for £47.07. The break-even against the 90% boiler falls to a COP of about 1.6, though only for the heat you manage to make in the cheap windows. The more your home holds its heat, the more of it you can do that way. [Insulating](/blog/draught-proofing-on-a-budget-uk/) and draught-proofing help here, and so does a lower flow temperature.

Rates move, and the cheap windows are only worth having if you can actually use them. Check the current rates on any tariff before you decide.

## The Boiler Upgrade Scheme

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is a government grant towards the cost of a heat pump. These are the terms as GOV.UK gave them on 4 November 2026 ([Apply for the Boiler Upgrade Scheme](https://www.gov.uk/apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme)):

- It is open to people in England and Wales. In Scotland, [Home Energy Scotland](https://www.homeenergyscotland.org/find-funding-grants-and-loans/interest-free-loans/overview/) has interest-free loans and grants. The Northern Ireland boiler replacement scheme is closed.
- £7,500 towards an air source heat pump, £7,500 towards a ground source heat pump, and £2,500 towards an air-to-air heat pump.
- Until March 2027, a further £1,500 towards an air or ground source heat pump if your home is heated by oil or LPG and has no mains gas connection.
- No grant for a hybrid system, such as a gas boiler combined with a heat pump.

To be eligible, you must own the property and be replacing a fossil fuel heating system, which includes gas, oil, LPG and electric. You can still get it if you have had funding for insulation before. Most new builds, social housing and properties already given government funding for a heat pump are excluded, so read [the full eligibility page](https://www.gov.uk/apply-boiler-upgrade-scheme/check-if-youre-eligible).

You do not apply yourself. You get quotes from MCS certified installers, agree one, and the installer applies to Ofgem for you. The grant is taken off what you pay, and shows on the quote and invoice. The heat pump must be installed and commissioned within 120 days of the application.

Schemes change, so check the GOV.UK page before you commit to anything.

## What to ask before you buy

1. **The seasonal efficiency figure** for your home, and how the installer worked it out.
2. **A heat loss calculation** for each room, so the system is sized for your home.
3. **Whether your radiators and hot water cylinder are suitable**, and what changes would cost.
4. **The full price after the grant**, with the grant shown as a line on the quote.
5. **Which tariffs suit it.** A heat pump on a time-of-use tariff is a different sum from one on a standard tariff.

GOV.UK also has a short [heat pump suitability check](https://www.gov.uk/check-heat-pump).

## The short version

- A heat pump costs less to run than gas only if its COP beats the break-even, about 2.9 against a 90% boiler at the 4 November 2026 Flexible Octopus prices in London.
- Cheap-rate windows on a time-of-use tariff lower that bar a lot, if you can use them.
- The Boiler Upgrade Scheme gives £7,500 towards an air source heat pump in England and Wales, taken off the installer's quote.
- Get the seasonal efficiency figure in writing.
