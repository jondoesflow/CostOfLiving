Short answer: **if you dry three or more loads a week and your old dryer has given up, yes. A heat pump dryer uses about 1.5 kWh a load against about 4 kWh for a vented or condenser dryer. On a flat tariff in Yorkshire on 23 November 2026, that saved about 64p a load, or about £100 a year at three loads a week.**

If your current dryer still works and you only use it now and then, it's a different story. The saving is real, but it can take the best part of a decade to earn back what you paid.

Here are the sums, so you can see where you land.

## What each one costs to run

Cheap Watts uses the same typical figures everywhere on the site: **4 kWh for a full load in a vented or condenser dryer, and 1.5 kWh for a full load in a heat pump dryer, three loads a week.**

At the Flexible Octopus rate for Yorkshire on 23 November 2026, 25.60p per kWh:

| Dryer | Per load | Three loads a week, for a year |
|---|---|---|
| Vented or condenser | about £1.02 | about £159.76 |
| Heat pump | about 38p | about £59.91 |
| The difference | about 64p | about £99.85 |

Which? gets to a similar place by its own testing. In its [guide to heat pump tumble dryers](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2021/03/why-heat-pump-tumble-dryers-cost-less-to-run-and-use-less-energy), it puts the heat pump dryers it has tested at "around £54 per year to run" and condenser dryers at "£139 a year" on average, at three loads a week. Different machines and a different price per unit, but the same shape: a heat pump dryer costs roughly a third as much to run.

Your own machine might use more or less than the typical figure. The energy label or the manual will give you a better number for your model.

## Does where you live change the answer?

A little. Electricity costs more in some regions than others, so the saving is bigger where it's dearer. These are the Flexible Octopus unit rates from the [Octopus price data](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/) on 23 November 2026, for the 2.5 kWh you save on each load.

| Region | Price per kWh | Saved per load | Saved a year at three loads a week |
|---|---|---|---|
| East Midlands (cheapest) | 25.36p | 63.4p | £98.89 |
| Yorkshire | 25.60p | 64.0p | £99.85 |
| Merseyside and North Wales (dearest) | 27.86p | 69.7p | £108.66 |

So somewhere around £100 to £110 a year, depending on the region. How often you use the dryer matters far more than where you live, which brings us to payback.

## How long does it take to pay back?

This is the bit that decides it. A heat pump dryer costs more to buy. Which? says they're "often a couple of hundred pounds more expensive than vented or condenser dryers", and in the same guide gives average prices of £661 for a heat pump dryer and £307 for a condenser. That's a gap of £354 between the averages. Which? puts the average vented dryer at £261, so if that's what you'd otherwise buy, the gap is £400 and payback takes a little longer. Shop around and you may well pay less, but I'll use those averages because they're the figures I can point to.

There are two very different situations here.

**Your old dryer has died.** You're buying a dryer either way, so the only extra cost is the gap: about £354.

**Your old dryer still works.** You'd be paying the full price of the new one, about £661, purely to save on running costs.

Here's how long each takes to earn back at the Yorkshire rate on 23 November 2026, depending on how many loads you dry:

| Loads a week | Saved a year | Paying back £354 (old dryer dead) | Paying back £661 (old dryer working) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One | £33.28 | about 10.6 years | about 19.9 years |
| Three | £99.85 | about 3.5 years | about 6.6 years |
| Five | £166.42 | about 2.1 years | about 4.0 years |

That table is the whole article really.

If your dryer is broken and you use one most days, a heat pump dryer pays its extra cost back in two to four years and then keeps saving. That's an easy call.

If your dryer works fine and you only run it once a week for towels, you'd be waiting the best part of 20 years to get your money back. Keep the one you've got and spend the money elsewhere.

Everyone in between needs to look at their own numbers. Count how many loads you actually dry in a normal week, not a bad week in January, and find your row.

## What you give up

A heat pump dryer isn't a straight swap. Which? is clear that "Heat pump dryers work at lower temperatures, so drying takes longer but is less damaging to fabrics", and that "They can often take longer to dry clothes than other dryer types."

The site's typical figures reflect that: two hours for a conventional dryer load and two and a half for a heat pump dryer. Your machine might differ. If you rely on getting a load dry in a hurry before school or work, that extra half hour is worth knowing about before you buy.

The upside of the lower temperature is gentler drying. If you've ever shrunk a jumper in a tumble dryer, that counts for something.

## Is a time-of-use tariff a better fix?

It's a different fix, and it works with either dryer. On Octopus Go in Yorkshire on 23 November 2026, the cheap rate was 9.40p per kWh from 12.30am to 5.30am, and 33.06p the rest of the day. That makes a conventional dryer load about 38p in the cheap hours and about £1.32 outside them.

The catch is that the cheap hours are the middle of the night, and [Devon and Somerset Fire and Rescue](https://www.dsfire.gov.uk/news/safety/why-you-shouldnt-use-washing-machines-and-tumble-dryers-night) says: "The safest time to use electricals is when you are awake, and definitely in the house." I wouldn't run a tumble dryer of any kind while everyone's asleep.

It also cuts both ways. On a cheaper unit rate, the heat pump dryer saves you less each load, so it takes longer to pay back. On Go's cheap rate, the 2.5 kWh you save on each load is worth about 23.5p rather than 64p.

## The cheaper option most people forget

Before you spend £661 on any dryer, it's worth asking whether you need to tumble dry everything. A heated airer uses about 1.2 kWh for a four hour session on the site's figures, which was about 31p in Yorkshire on 23 November 2026. That's cheaper than either dryer, though it's slower and puts the water from your washing into the room.

I went through all the options side by side, including dehumidifiers, in [Heated airer vs tumble dryer: which is cheaper to run?](/blog/heated-airer-vs-tumble-dryer-running-cost/) For a lot of homes, an airer for everyday loads and a dryer for towels and bedding is cheaper than any single machine.

## The short version

- A heat pump dryer uses about 1.5 kWh a load against about 4 kWh for a vented or condenser dryer. That saved about 64p a load in Yorkshire on 23 November 2026.
- At three loads a week that's about £100 a year, a little more in the dearer regions.
- If your old dryer has died, the extra cost of a heat pump dryer pays back in about three and a half years at three loads a week.
- If your old dryer still works, replacing it early only makes sense if you dry a lot.
- Expect longer drying times, and don't run any dryer overnight while you're asleep.

This week, count how many dryer loads you actually do. Then find your row in the payback table. That one number answers the question better than any advert will.
