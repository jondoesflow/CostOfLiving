The Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS) has closed, so you can't apply to it any more. Free or subsidised insulation is not gone, though. ECO4 is still running until 31 December 2026, and in England the Warm Homes: Local Grant is open through your council.

Which one fits depends on your income, your benefits and where you live. I'll go through each, say what I could and couldn't confirm, and finish with the order I'd try them in. I checked every page below on 31 October 2026.

## Is the Great British Insulation Scheme still open?

No. [Ofgem's scheme page](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/great-british-Insulation-scheme) says it "began in April 2023 and ended on 31 March 2026", and that it is "now closed to new installations".

If an advert, a leaflet or a caller says you can still apply to GBIS, be wary. Ask who they are and check with Ofgem or your council before you give anyone your details.

## Is ECO4 still open?

Yes, for now. The [Energy Company Obligation](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/energy-company-obligation-eco) is the scheme where energy suppliers fund home upgrades for people in fuel poverty. Ofgem says ECO4 "will run until 31 December 2026, following a nine-month extension".

Two things to know before you get your hopes up. Ofgem's page says "ECO is not a grant scheme", and that eligibility for a measure "does not guarantee it will be installed". Suppliers decide which measures to fund and how much, and you may be asked to contribute. I couldn't find a published figure for how much funding is left, so don't assume the money is there.

## Who qualifies for ECO4?

[Ofgem's guidance for homeowners and tenants](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/energy-company-obligation-eco/homeowners-and-tenants) gives two main routes. Your home must need the upgrades, a retrofit assessment decides which, and you must own it or have your landlord's permission. Ofgem's [FAQ](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/energy-company-obligation-eco/homeowners-and-tenants/faqs-domestic-consumers-and-landlords) says only homes with an EPC rating of D to G can be treated, and privately rented homes need an E, F or G.

| Route | Who it covers |
|---|---|
| Benefits | Someone in the household gets Child Benefit (within income caps, which Ofgem sets out in its ECO4 delivery guidance), Pension Guarantee Credit, income-related ESA, income-based JSA, Income Support, Universal Credit, Housing Benefit or Pension Credit Savings Credit |
| ECO4 Flex | Your council or devolved government refers you. Combined gross household income under £31,000, or someone in the household has a severe or long-term health condition that is adversely affected by living in a cold home, due to a cardiovascular condition, respiratory disease, limited mobility or immunosuppression |

This table is a short guide, not the full rulebook. Suppliers can also refer households with persistent fuel debt or prepayment meter problems, and Ofgem says councils can use other criteria to identify low-income or vulnerable households. Social homes let below the market rate can also be treated if their EPC rating is D to G (see Ofgem's [FAQ](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/energy-company-obligation-eco/homeowners-and-tenants/faqs-domestic-consumers-and-landlords)), so social tenants should ask their landlord.

## How do you apply for ECO4?

1. **Contact an obligated energy supplier.** Ofgem says you can do this even if they aren't your supplier, and its page links to a contact list.
2. **For ECO4 Flex, ask your local council** whether it takes part. Some allow you to refer yourself.
3. **Use a TrustMark installer.** Ofgem says installers must be TrustMark accredited, so ask to see their registration details.

Time matters here. With the end date two months away, I'd make the call this week rather than in the new year.

## The Warm Homes: Local Grant (England only)

This is the route more households can use. On [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/apply-warm-homes-local-grant), the Warm Homes: Local Grant is for homes in England that are privately owned (by you or your landlord) with an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating of D, E, F or G.

- **Income:** household income must usually be £36,000 a year or less. Higher earners may still qualify if they live in certain postcode areas or someone in the household gets certain benefits.
- **What it can pay for:** wall, loft and underfloor insulation, air source heat pumps, smart controls and solar panels. The council arranges and pays for the agreed work, so you don't pay. A landlord may need to pay for some improvements.
- **Funding:** eligibility alone isn't enough, because your council must have funding available.
- **How to apply:** use the "Start now" link on the GOV.UK page, or call 0800 098 7950 (Monday to Friday 8am to 6pm, Saturday 9am to 12pm). GOV.UK says your council usually contacts you within 10 working days to arrange a home survey.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have separate schemes, which the GOV.UK page links to.

## What about the Warm Homes Plan and the Budget?

The Budget on 28 October 2026 saved £1 billion of capital grant from the Warm Homes Plan, but the government says "the majority of WHP funding for low-income households and social housing" is protected. I covered this in my [Autumn Budget post](/blog/autumn-budget-2026-energy-bills-what-to-expect/). I haven't found anything yet that says what it means for a particular scheme or council, so I'm not going to guess.

## If you don't qualify for any of it

You can still do the cheap jobs yourself. My [draught-proofing guide](/blog/draught-proofing-on-a-budget-uk/) covers the fixes that cost little and need no tradesperson. For advice on which upgrades suit your home, GOV.UK's energy adviser line is 0800 098 7950, on the [improve your home's energy efficiency](https://www.gov.uk/improve-energy-efficiency) page.

Insulation only pays off if the heat you save is worth something, and that depends on how you heat the house. The [Gas or electric?](/gas-or-electric/) tool shows what each unit of heat costs you at current prices.

## The short version

- GBIS closed on 31 March 2026.
- ECO4 runs to 31 December 2026. Qualifying usually means certain benefits, or a council referral on income under £31,000 or a health condition. Funding isn't guaranteed.
- In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant is open if your home is D to G on its EPC and household income is usually £36,000 or less, subject to your council having funding.
- Ask for TrustMark registration before anyone touches your home.

This week, check your EPC rating and apply through the GOV.UK Warm Homes page. It takes a few minutes and gets you in the queue.
