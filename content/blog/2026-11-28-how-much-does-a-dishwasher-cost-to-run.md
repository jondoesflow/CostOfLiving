Short answer: **an eco programme uses about 1 kWh, which cost about 26p on a standard flat tariff in Yorkshire on 19 November 2026. Five runs a week comes to about £66.57 a year.**

When you run it only matters if you're on a tariff where the price changes through the day. On a flat tariff, 2pm and 2am cost exactly the same. On a time-of-use tariff, the same load ran from about 9p to 54p on the day I checked, depending on the hour.

Here's the sum, what the timing is worth, and the safety point that should shape when you press start.

## The sum for one run

Cheap Watts uses the same typical figure for a dishwasher everywhere on the site: **1.0 kWh for an eco programme, which takes about two and a half hours, five times a week.**

The cost of a run is:

**kWh per run × your price per kWh = the cost of the run**

At the Flexible Octopus Direct Debit rate for Yorkshire on 19 November 2026, 25.60p per kWh, that's 25.60p a run. Five runs a week is 260 a year, or 260 kWh, which comes to **£66.57 a year**.

Your own machine will differ. The energy label shows the eco programme's use in kWh per 100 cycles, so a label showing 80 kWh per 100 cycles means about 0.8 kWh a run. I went through how to turn any label into pounds in [Energy labels explained](/blog/energy-labels-explained-a-to-g-pounds-per-year/). Bear in mind the label is tested on the eco programme. If you use a hotter or quicker one, expect to use more.

## What a run costs on different tariffs

These are the Direct Debit unit rates for Yorkshire from the [Octopus price data](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/) on 19 November 2026, using the 1.0 kWh eco run.

| Tariff and time | Price per kWh | One run | Five a week for a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexible Octopus (flat), any time | 25.60p | 25.6p | £66.57 |
| Octopus Go, 12.30am to 5.30am | 9.40p | 9.4p | £24.44 |
| Octopus Go, rest of the day | 33.06p | 33.1p | £85.95 |
| Cosy Octopus, cheap hours | 13.59p | 13.6p | £35.35 |
| Cosy Octopus, standard hours | 27.72p | 27.7p | £72.07 |
| Cosy Octopus, 4pm to 7pm | 41.58p | 41.6p | £108.11 |
| Agile Octopus, cheapest half hour | 27.74p | 27.7p | n/a |
| Agile Octopus, dearest half hour | 54.20p | 54.2p | n/a |

On Cosy Octopus the cheap hours that day were 4am to 7am, 1pm to 4pm and 10pm to midnight. Agile changes every half hour. On 19 November its cheapest slot was 3.30am and its dearest was 5.30pm, and every half hour published for that day was above the flat rate, so a winter week on Agile can cost more than a flat tariff for the same use.

Two things worth knowing before you look at that table and think about switching:

- Octopus describes Go as "the smart EV tariff" and Cosy as "a heat pump tariff", so check whether you qualify before you plan around them.
- Time-of-use tariffs charge more outside their cheap hours. Shifting the dishwasher alone won't pay for a tariff change if the rest of your evening use costs more, so price the whole day, not one appliance.

## The worst time to run it

If your tariff has a peak, it's usually teatime. On Cosy Octopus, 4pm to 7pm was 41.58p. On Agile on 19 November, every half hour from 4pm to 7pm was above 53p.

That's exactly when most of us load the dishwasher, straight after tea. On those tariffs, waiting until after 7pm is the easiest saving you'll make: on Cosy, it takes a run from 41.6p to 27.7p, without changing anything else.

On a flat tariff, none of this applies. Run it whenever suits you.

## The safety point: don't run it while you sleep

The cheapest hours on most tariffs are the middle of the night. That's a problem, because fire services such as Devon and Somerset advise against running appliances while you're asleep or out.

[Devon and Somerset Fire and Rescue](https://www.dsfire.gov.uk/news/safety/why-you-shouldnt-use-washing-machines-and-tumble-dryers-night) puts it plainly: "The safest time to use electricals is when you are awake, and definitely in the house." It includes the dishwasher in that advice and says "You should never leave appliances on when you're out." If a fire starts at night, it can develop for longer before anyone notices.

It also suggests closing the door to the room where the appliance is running and checking your smoke alarms work. A kitchen needs a heat alarm rather than a smoke alarm.

So the cheapest slot isn't always the right one. Here's how that plays out on each tariff:

- **Flat tariff.** Timing doesn't change the price, so run it while you're up and about.
- **Octopus Go.** The cheap window ends at 5.30am. If you're up early, start it before 5.30am so the first part of the cycle, where much of the water heating happens, gets the cheap rate, and you're awake for the rest.
- **Cosy Octopus.** The 1pm to 4pm cheap window is in the daytime, so if someone is home in the afternoon you get the cheap rate and stay safe.
- **Agile.** Look at the day's prices and pick the cheapest slot when you're awake. Late evening is often cheaper than teatime.

## Where the electricity actually goes

Most of a dishwasher's electricity goes on heating water, early in the cycle and again near the end, and that's how Cheap Watts counts it. Pumping and spraying the water uses far less.

That's also why an eco programme takes longer: it heats the water less and makes up for it with time. The long run time isn't what costs you money, the heat is.

## How to cut the cost

1. **Use the eco programme.** It's the one the energy label is tested on, so it's the figure you can compare, even if it takes hours.
2. **Run it less often.** Waiting for a full load means fewer runs. Going from five runs a week to four saves 52 kWh a year, which is about £13.31 at the flat rate above.
3. **Skip the heated dry if you can.** Some machines let you turn it off. Opening the door at the end lets the dishes dry in their own heat.
4. **Scrape, don't rinse.** Rinsing plates under the hot tap first means paying to heat water twice.
5. **Measure your own machine.** An energy monitoring plug shows what a run really uses, and you can swap that number into the sum above instead of the typical 1 kWh.
