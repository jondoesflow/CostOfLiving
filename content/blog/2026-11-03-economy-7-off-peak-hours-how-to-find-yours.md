Short answer: **Economy 7 gives you seven hours of cheaper electricity every night, and the exact hours are set by your supplier and area, so look at your bill or ask them. It only pays if roughly a third or more of your electricity is used in those hours.**

On a Flexible Octopus Economy 7 tariff in London on 3 November 2026, the night rate is 13.99p per kWh and the day rate is 33.11p. A standard single-rate tariff is 26.60p. This post shows how to find your hours, how to check whether you are ahead, and which jobs are worth moving.

## Where the numbers come from

The prices are from [Octopus Energy's public API](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/VAR-22-11-01/), read on 3 November 2026. They are Direct Debit rates for London (region C), including VAT, for Flexible Octopus. Other suppliers and regions will differ, and I have used one product as the example, so treat the pence as illustrative and swap in your own from your bill.

| Tariff (London, Direct Debit) | Unit rate | Standing charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economy 7 day rate | 33.11p per kWh | 40.96p a day |
| Economy 7 night rate | 13.99p per kWh | (same charge) |
| Single rate | 26.60p per kWh | 41.39p a day |

The day rate is about 2.4 times the night rate. That gap is the whole game: cheap at night, dearer than a standard tariff by day.

## How do you find your Economy 7 hours?

There are three reliable routes, easiest first.

1. **Your bill or online account.** Your supplier sets the times, so they should be listed there. If not, ask them.
2. **Ring or message your supplier.** [EDF says](https://www.edfenergy.com/energywise/all-you-need-know-about-economy-7) your times vary with where you live and who your supplier is, and that you usually get seven hours of off-peak electricity between 11pm and 8am, sometimes split into chunks. Its advice is to ask your supplier for the exact times.
3. **Check the meter yourself.** An Economy 7 meter has two sets of numbers, day and night (often shown as low and high, or Rate 1 and Rate 2). Write down both readings just before and just after the time you think the cheap rate starts, and see which one moved. This is my practical method rather than an official one, but it settles the question.

One more thing from the same EDF page: ask how the clocks changing affects your times. The meter does not normally change its own clock, so your off-peak window can sit an hour differently in British Summer Time than in Greenwich Mean Time. The clocks went back on 25 October, so if you set a timer last summer, check it still lines up.

## Is Economy 7 actually cheaper for you?

Here is the break-even sum on the London rates above. I compare unit rates only, because the standing charges are almost the same.

- Day rate minus single rate: 33.11 - 26.60 = 6.51p
- Day rate minus night rate: 33.11 - 13.99 = 19.12p
- 6.51 divided by 19.12 = **about 34%**

So if more than about a third of your electricity is used in the off-peak window, Economy 7 beats the single rate on these prices. Less than that, and you lose. I ran the same sum for all 14 regions on the same product and the break-even landed between 33.7% and 34.7% each time, so a third is a decent rule of thumb for this tariff.

To show what that means in pounds, take a household using 3,000 kWh a year. That figure is an assumption for the example, not an official average. Standing charges are included.

| Share used at night | Economy 7 a year | Single rate a year | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20% | £1,028 | £949 | £79 worse |
| 30% | £971 | £949 | £22 worse |
| 40% | £913 | £949 | £36 better |
| 50% | £856 | £949 | £93 better |
| 60% | £799 | £949 | £150 better |

These are my sums from the day, night and standing rates above, over 365 days. Your usage and your tariff will move them.

Notice the downside is real. At 20% night use you would pay about £79 more. If your habits have changed since you took Economy 7, it is worth checking before the winter bills arrive.

## Who it suits

Economy 7 is built for homes that heat with electricity overnight, such as storage heaters or a hot water cylinder with an immersion heater, because that is a lot of energy used while you sleep. It can also work for people who are out all day and do their laundry and cooking late. If you are home all day with the heating and kettle on, it probably does not suit.

I have not found a sourced figure for how many UK homes still use Economy 7, so I will not guess.

## Which jobs to move into the cheap hours

The saving per kWh moved is 19.12p. The appliance figures below are the typical ones on the Cheap Watts site, so these match what the [calculator](/) shows. The yearly figures assume you shift every use to the cheap window.

| Job | kWh per use | Saved per use | Uses per week | Saved a year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dishwasher, eco programme | 1.0 | 19p | 5 | about £50 |
| Washing machine, 40°C | 0.8 | 15p | 4 | about £32 |
| Tumble dryer | 4.0 | 76p | 3 | about £119 |
| Immersion heater, an hour a day | 3.0 | 57p | 7 | about £209 |

Do not add the column together and promise yourself £400. Some of those jobs may already happen at night, and a household that moves everything will often find it needs to rethink when the immersion switches on. The immersion line is also the least realistic, because it assumes a full hour of heating every day, which depends on how much hot water you use.

A safety point before you set anything running. Fire services advise against leaving washing machines, tumble dryers and dishwashers running overnight or while you are out, because if something goes wrong you have far less time to react. [Devon and Somerset Fire and Rescue](https://www.dsfire.gov.uk/news/safety/why-you-shouldnt-use-washing-machines-and-tumble-dryers-night) has said so publicly, and I cover it in [the cheapest time to use electricity](/blog/cheapest-time-to-use-electricity-uk/).

So use the cheap hours while you are awake and at home where you can. Many off-peak windows run late in the evening or into the early morning, which helps. Items built to run unattended, such as storage heaters and a hot water cylinder on a timer, are the natural fit for the overnight rate. Check your own manual before using a delay start or plug-in timer on anything else.

1. Find your off-peak hours today, from your bill or supplier.
