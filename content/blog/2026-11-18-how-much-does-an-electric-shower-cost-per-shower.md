How much does an electric shower cost per shower? **A 9.5kW electric shower costs about 34p for an eight-minute shower, which is roughly 4p a minute. A 7.5kW shower costs about 27p and a 10.5kW one about 37p, on the prices below.** Have one a day and a 9.5kW shower adds up to about £123 a year per person.

Here is the sum, how to check it against your own shower, and why the same hot water from a gas boiler costs a lot less.

## The price I used

This is the Flexible Octopus standard electricity rate for London (region C), including VAT, from the Octopus API on 9 November 2026:

| Fuel | Unit rate |
|---|---|
| Electricity | 26.6p per kWh |
| Gas | 8.15p per kWh |

Your region and supplier will differ a little. The [Cheap Watts](/) calculator uses live prices for your own area. If you want to know where the 26.6p comes from, I broke it down in my post on the [Ofgem price cap](/blog/ofgem-price-cap-explained-how-it-affects-your-bill/).

## What each shower rating costs

An electric shower heats the water as it flows, so it draws its full rating the whole time it is running. The kilowatt figure is on the shower's label or in the manual. Multiply it by the hours you run it, then by the unit rate.

For an eight-minute shower (0.133 of an hour):

| Shower rating | Energy used | Cost per shower | Cost per year, one a day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7.5kW | 1.0 kWh | 27p | £97 |
| 8.5kW | 1.13 kWh | 30p | £110 |
| 9.5kW | 1.27 kWh | 34p | £123 |
| 10.5kW | 1.4 kWh | 37p | £136 |

The 9.5kW row matches the typical electric shower figure Cheap Watts uses: 1.27 kWh for eight minutes.

Two things to know about these figures:

- **The rating is the maximum.** Many showers draw less on a cooler setting, so your real cost may be a little lower. The label is the safe number to plan with.
- **A bigger number does not mean a worse deal.** More kilowatts heats more water per minute to the same temperature. It is the minutes that cost you money.

## Every minute costs the same

A 9.5kW shower uses about 0.158 kWh a minute, which is about 4.2p a minute at 26.6p. That makes it easy to see what a change in habit is worth:

- One minute shorter, once a day, saves about £15 a year.
- Four minutes instead of eight saves about £62 a year, at about 17p a shower.
- A household of four each showering once a day for eight minutes spends about £490 a year on shower electricity.

That last figure is just four times the single-person sum. It assumes everyone has one shower a day, so adjust for your own house.

## Why an electric shower is not very powerful

This is my own working, using the textbook fact that it takes about 4.19 kJ to heat a litre of water by 1°C. It is an illustration, not a measurement of your shower.

Suppose the water arrives at 10°C and you want it at 38°C, a 28°C rise. A 9.5kW shower can heat about 4.9 litres a minute to that temperature (heating one litre by 28°C takes 4.19 x 28 = 117 kJ, which is 1.95 kW for a litre a minute, and 9.5 divided by 1.95 is about 4.9). In a cold winter the incoming water is colder, so the same shower delivers less flow at the same temperature. That is why electric showers can feel weaker in January than in July.

## Electric vs gas: the same hot water costs far less from a boiler

At eight minutes, a 9.5kW shower heats about 39 litres of water by 28°C, using 1.27 kWh of electricity.

Heating that same 39 litres with a gas boiler needs the same 1.27 kWh of heat. I have assumed the boiler turns 85% of its gas into heat. That is my working figure, not a measurement of your boiler.

- Gas needed: 1.27 divided by 0.85 = about 1.49 kWh
- Cost: 1.49 x 8.15p = **about 12p**, against 34p from the electric shower

So the same heat costs roughly a third as much from gas. This is not a reason to rip out an electric shower. Many homes have no gas, and a gas-fed mixer or power shower often uses more water per minute than an electric one, which eats into the saving. But if you are choosing between the two for a new bathroom, running cost favours gas, and the [gas or electric](/gas-or-electric/) tool lets you test that with your own prices.

## Can you run it in the cheap hours?

Not really. An electric shower heats water at the moment you use it, so a cheap overnight rate only helps if you shower at that time. That is the difference from an immersion heater, which heats a tank you can schedule. If you are on a time-of-use tariff, the [Cheap Watts tool](/) shows your cheapest hours, but for a shower the practical lever is time, not timing.

## What to do next

1. Find your shower's kW rating on the label or in the manual.
2. Multiply by the minutes you shower, divided by 60, then by your unit rate.
3. Time a typical shower. Most people guess low.
4. Try shaving a minute or two off. A shower timer in the bathroom makes this easy to stick to.

Four minutes is a target, not a rule. If you only manage six, you have still saved about £31 a year per daily shower on the prices above.
