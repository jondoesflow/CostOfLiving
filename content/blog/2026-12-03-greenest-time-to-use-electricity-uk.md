Short answer: **the grid is usually greenest overnight, roughly 11pm to 5am, and dirtiest in the late afternoon and early evening. But the weather matters more than the clock. A windy day can be cleaner at teatime than a still day is at 3am.**

And yes, the greenest time is often the cheapest, if your tariff lets it be. I lined up two weeks of the grid's carbon figures against Agile Octopus prices, and the greenest quarter of the half hours cost about a third less than the dirtiest quarter.

Here's what the numbers showed.

## What "greenest" actually means

Every unit of electricity you use comes from a mix of power stations: wind, solar, nuclear, biomass, gas and power brought in from abroad. The more gas in the mix, the more carbon dioxide behind each unit. That's measured as grams of CO2 per kWh, called the carbon intensity.

The figures here come from the [Carbon Intensity API](https://carbonintensity.org.uk/), run by the National Energy System Operator (NESO) with partners including the University of Oxford and WWF. It forecasts the carbon intensity for Great Britain, and for 14 regions, every half hour, two days ahead. It's the same forecast Cheap Watts uses.

NESO's own explanation is the useful bit: carbon intensity "varies by hour, day, and season due to changes in electricity demand, low carbon generation (wind, solar, hydro, nuclear, biomass) and conventional generation."

## Greenest and dirtiest times of day

I took the national figures for every half hour from 10 to 23 November 2026, 672 of them, and averaged them by the hour they started. Here's a selection, with the average Agile Octopus price in Yorkshire for the same hours.

| Time (UK) | Average carbon | Average Agile price |
|---|---|---|
| 3am | 133g per kWh | 28.8p |
| 8am | 175g per kWh | 38.6p |
| Noon | 164g per kWh | 34.9p |
| 4pm | 193g per kWh | 57.0p |
| 5pm | 192g per kWh | 57.9p |
| 9pm | 160g per kWh | 33.4p |
| 11pm | 134g per kWh | 30.3p |

The pattern is clear enough. Overnight, when demand is low, the grid is cleanest and Agile is cheapest. From about 3pm to 7pm, when the country gets home and puts the kettle and the oven on, carbon is at its highest, and from 4pm Agile prices jump.

That's the November picture. NESO says carbon intensity also varies by season, so check the forecast rather than relying on these hours all year.

## The weather matters more than the clock

Averages hide how much this swings. Over those same two weeks:

- The cleanest half hour was 1:30am on 16 November, at **60g per kWh**.
- The dirtiest was 7am on 10 November, at **267g per kWh**. More than four times as much.
- The greenest whole day was 17 November, averaging 113g. The dirtiest was 10 November, averaging 245g.

Wind is the big swing factor. When I checked at 9am on 24 November 2026, wind was producing 44.2% of Great Britain's electricity and gas 35.9%. Yorkshire's forecast for the rest of that day stayed between 27g and 66g per kWh, even through the evening peak, which is cleaner than the overnight average in the table above.

So "run it at 3am" is a decent rule of thumb, but on a windy day you may not need to wait at all. Checking the forecast beats guessing.

## Is the greenest time the cheapest?

Often, not always. On Agile Octopus, the price changes every half hour with the wholesale market, so it's the tariff where the two are most closely linked. Across the 672 half hours from 10 to 23 November 2026, in Yorkshire:

- The greenest quarter of half hours averaged 29.6p a unit on Agile. The dirtiest quarter averaged 43.9p.
- The cheapest quarter of half hours averaged 129g per kWh. The dearest quarter averaged 203g per kWh.

Put another way, the greenest half hours cost about a third less than the dirtiest, and the cheapest half hours had about a third less carbon than the dearest.

They don't line up perfectly. On 24 November 2026 the greenest forecast slot in Yorkshire was 8:30pm to 9:30pm at 27g, while the cheapest Agile slot published for the rest of that day was 10:30pm at 30.66p. Close, but not the same half hour. When the price and the carbon pull in different directions, pick the one you care about more.

## What it means on your tariff

**On a standard flat tariff**, which most households are on, you pay the same price all day. Flexible Octopus in Yorkshire was 25.60p a unit on 24 November 2026, so a washing machine cycle costs about 20.5p whenever you start it. The time makes no difference to your bill, which makes it a free choice: you can run things at the greenest time and it costs you nothing.

**On Agile**, greener and cheaper mostly travel together, so chasing one usually gets you some of the other.

**On Economy 7, Octopus Go, EDF GoElectric or E.ON Next Drive**, the cheap hours are overnight, which is also when the grid tends to be cleanest. My earlier post on [the cheapest time to use electricity](/blog/cheapest-time-to-use-electricity-uk/) has the cheap hours for each.

## How much carbon does moving one load save?

Smaller than you might hope for one appliance, bigger for the heavy ones. Using the typical figures on Cheap Watts and the cleanest and dirtiest half hours from those two weeks:

| Appliance | Typical use | At 60g per kWh | At 267g per kWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Washing machine, 40°C wash | 0.8 kWh | 48g | 214g |
| Tumble dryer, full load | 4 kWh | 240g | 1.07kg |
| Electric car, 30 kWh charge | 30 kWh | 1.8kg | 8.0kg |

Those are the extremes, so most of the time the gap is smaller. The honest takeaway: shifting the odd wash won't change much on its own. Shifting the big, flexible loads, like car charging, the tumble dryer or an immersion heater, makes a real difference, and those are also where the money is on a time-of-use tariff.

## Be sensible about overnight

The greenest hours are often while you're asleep, but fire services advise against leaving washing machines, tumble dryers and dishwashers running overnight or while you're out. [Devon and Somerset Fire and Rescue](https://www.dsfire.gov.uk/news/safety/why-you-shouldnt-use-washing-machines-and-tumble-dryers-night) explains why. A late evening start, before you go to bed, or an early start when you get up, usually catches a clean patch while you're still around.
