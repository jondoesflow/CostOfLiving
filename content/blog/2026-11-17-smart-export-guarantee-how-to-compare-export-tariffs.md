The Smart Export Guarantee (SEG) is the scheme that pays you for the spare electricity your solar panels send back to the grid. You can choose the company that pays you, and the rates vary a lot. On the public tariffs I checked on 8 November 2026, the rate for each unit exported runs from 4.1p to 17.5p per kWh.

On 1,500 kWh exported in a year, that is £61.50 at the bottom and £262.50 at the top. Same panels, same sunshine, a £201 gap, just from the tariff you pick.

## How the SEG works

The SEG launched on 1 January 2020. [Ofgem explains](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/smart-export-guarantee-seg) the main rules:

- Suppliers called SEG licensees must pay small-scale generators for low-carbon electricity they export, if certain criteria are met.
- The eligible technologies are solar PV, wind, hydro, anaerobic digestion and micro-CHP. Installations must be in Great Britain, up to 5MW (or up to 50kW for micro-CHP).
- Each licensee sets its own rate, contract length and terms. The rate must always be above zero.
- You are paid on export meter readings.

The part people miss is on Ofgem's [page for generators](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/environmental-and-social-schemes/smart-export-guarantee-seg/smart-export-guarantee-seg-generators): you can apply with any SEG licensee, and it does not have to be the company that supplies your electricity or gas. You can split them up if you want.

Ofgem also says generators should shop around, and licensees must offer a tariff to all eligible generators. Nobody is forced to take whatever their supplier offers.

## What some tariffs pay

These are rates from the suppliers' public APIs, checked on 8 November 2026, except E.ON Next Export Premium v3, which comes from [E.ON Next's SEG page](https://www.eonnext.com/electricity-and-gas/smart-export-guarantee) read the same day. They are flat rates, the same in every region.

| Tariff | Export rate | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Octopus Outgoing Smart Export Guarantee | 4.1p per kWh | Fixed for 12 months, £100 exit fee |
| E.ON Next Flex Export v1 | 6.0p per kWh | Variable |
| Outgoing Octopus | 12.0p per kWh | Flat rate |
| E.ON Next Export Exclusive v3 | 13.0p per kWh | Fixed for 12 months |
| E.ON Next Export Premium v3 | 17.5p per kWh | Fixed for 12 months, one tariff per property |

This is not a full market list. Many suppliers offer SEG tariffs and I have only looked at the ones on these APIs.

E.ON Next says its tariffs need a smart export meter and consent to half-hourly data collection. Export Premium v3 is also limited to people with a solar or battery system installed by E.ON Installation Services from 10 November 2025. E.ON's page says it replaced Premium v2 on that date, so I have left the old 21p rate out. The APIs do not spell out conditions for the other tariffs, so read the supplier's own terms before assuming you qualify. A high headline rate can come with strings, such as needing to be a customer for import too, or to have a certain battery or smart tariff.

## What the gap is worth

I have assumed you export 1,500 kWh a year. That is my round number, not a measured figure: your own export meter readings will tell you the real one, and my [solar post](/blog/solar-panels-uk-how-much-generate-use-or-sell/) shows how the export amount depends on how much you use at home.

| Export rate | Value of 1,500 kWh |
|---|---|
| 4.1p | £61.50 |
| 6.0p | £90.00 |
| 12.0p | £180.00 |
| 13.0p | £195.00 |
| 17.5p | £262.50 |

Prices change and these sums assume the rate stays put for the year, which only fixed tariffs promise.

## Compare more than the headline rate

The highest number is not always the best deal. Check these:

1. **Fixed or variable.** A variable rate can fall. A fixed one is certain for its term, but you may be tied in.
2. **Exit fees.** The Octopus fixed tariff above carries a £100 exit fee. On a 4.1p rate with 1,500 kWh exported, that fee is bigger than a year of payments.
3. **Conditions.** Do you need to buy electricity from the same company? Is a particular meter, battery or tariff required?
4. **Contract length.** A 24 month fixed rate is great if you like it and annoying if something better turns up.
5. **How you are paid.** The terms say how and when payments reach you.

## Using your own power is still worth more

Even the best export rate here is below what you pay to buy electricity. The Ofgem price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026 is 26.32p per kWh for the Great Britain average on Direct Debit ([Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/news/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-october-and-31-december-2026)). Every unit you use yourself saves about 26p. Every unit you export earns somewhere between 4p and 17.5p on these tariffs.

So the order of play is: use as much of your solar as you sensibly can, then pick a good export tariff for the rest.

Running the dishwasher or washing machine at midday on a sunny day, or heating water with an immersion timer, moves units from the low-value column to the high-value one. Our [Plan my evening](/plan-my-evening/) tool shows what an appliance costs to run.

## How to switch export tariff

1. Find your export meter readings or your annual export total. If you have a smart meter, your app or your supplier can show it.
2. Look at SEG tariffs from the licensees on Ofgem's published list, and compare rate, term, exit fee and conditions.
3. Apply to the supplier you pick. Ofgem says you can choose any licensee.
4. Check your contract for any notice period before you leave your current one.
