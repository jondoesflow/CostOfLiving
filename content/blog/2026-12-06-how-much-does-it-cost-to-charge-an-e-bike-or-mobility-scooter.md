Short answer: **a full e-bike charge uses about half a unit of electricity, which cost about 13p on a standard flat tariff in Yorkshire on 27 November 2026. A full mobility scooter charge uses about one unit, or about 26p.**

Charged three times a week, that's about £20 a year for an e-bike and about £40 a year for a mobility scooter. Charging is one of the cheapest parts of owning either.

Where people go wrong isn't the cost. It's charging overnight to catch a cheap rate, which is exactly what fire services ask you not to do. So here are the sums, what the time of day does to the price, and how to keep the cost down without charging while you sleep.

## The sum for one charge

Cheap Watts uses the same typical figures across the site:

| What you're charging | Electricity per full charge | How long it takes | Charges a week |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-bike or e-scooter | 0.5 kWh | about 4 hours | 3 |
| Mobility scooter | 1.0 kWh | about 8 hours | 3 |

Both work out at an average of about 125 watts while charging. It's slow and steady, which is why it takes hours but costs so little.

The cost of a charge is:

**kWh per charge × your price per kWh = the cost of the charge**

At the Flexible Octopus Direct Debit rate for Yorkshire on 27 November 2026, 25.60p per kWh:

- **E-bike:** 0.5 × 25.60p = **about 13p a charge**. Three a week is 78 kWh a year, or **£19.97 a year**.
- **Mobility scooter:** 1.0 × 25.60p = **about 26p a charge**. Three a week is 156 kWh a year, or **£39.94 a year**.

If you ride your e-bike to work every day and charge it every night you get home, that's 182.5 kWh a year, or about £46.72 at the same rate. Still less than a pound a week.

These are typical figures, not yours. Batteries vary a lot in size, and a charge from half full uses half as much. If you want your real number, an energy monitoring plug between the charger and the socket shows exactly what a charge used.

## What the time of day does to the price

On a flat tariff, it doesn't matter when you plug in. On a time-of-use tariff, it does. These are the Direct Debit unit rates for Yorkshire from the [Octopus price data](https://api.octopus.energy/v1/products/) on 27 November 2026.

| Tariff and when you charge | Price per kWh | E-bike charge | Mobility scooter charge | Mobility scooter, a year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flexible Octopus (flat), any time | 25.60p | 13p | 26p | £39.94 |
| Cosy Octopus, cheap hours | 13.59p | 7p | 14p | £21.21 |
| Cosy Octopus, 4pm to 7pm peak | 41.58p | 21p | 42p | £64.87 |
| Octopus Go, daytime | 33.06p | 17p | 33p | £51.57 |
| Octopus Go, 12.30am to 5.30am | 9.40p | 5p | 9p | £14.66 |

On Cosy Octopus that day, the cheap hours were 4am to 7am, 1pm to 4pm and 10pm to midnight.

The cheapest row is the middle of the night, and that's the one I'd steer you away from. More on that below.

The difference across a year is real but small. The gap between a flat tariff and Go's night rate for a mobility scooter is about £25 a year. For an e-bike it's about half that. It's not a reason to change tariff on its own, and Octopus describes Go as "the smart EV tariff", so it's built around car charging. If you have an electric car as well, I covered that properly in [how much it costs to charge an electric car at home](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-charge-an-electric-car-at-home/).

Watch out for the daytime rate on Go, too. At 33.06p it's dearer than the flat tariff, so a mobility scooter charged during the day on Go costs more than it would on Flexible Octopus.

## Why not charge overnight?

Because the fire services say not to. [London Fire Brigade's charging advice](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/e-bikes-and-e-scooters/how-to-charge-e-bikes-and-e-scooters-safely/) says "Most fires involving e-bikes and e-scooters happen while the battery is charging" and "Never leave a battery charging unattended or while you're asleep."

[North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue's advice](https://www.northyorksfire.gov.uk/your-safety/safe-at-home/e-scooter-e-bike-and-mobility-scooter-fire-safety-advice/) covers e-scooters, e-bikes and mobility aids, and says "Never leave your appliance charging overnight or when you are away from the property".

I know that's awkward for a mobility scooter. A full charge takes most of a working day, and a lot of people rely on theirs being ready in the morning. But a few pence a night isn't worth the risk. Charge during the day while you're in and awake, and unplug it once it's full.

The same two pages also say to:

- use the charger that came with it, or one the manufacturer approves, and buy replacements only from reputable sellers
- follow the manufacturer's instructions, and unplug the charger once it's finished
- let the battery cool after a ride before you charge it
- charge on a hard, flat surface where the heat can get away, and don't cover the battery or charger
- never charge or store it on an escape route, in a hallway you'd need to get out through, or in a bedroom
- have a working smoke alarm where you charge
- stop using a battery that has been dropped or damaged

## Cheaper and safer: charge in the afternoon

If you're on a tariff with cheap daytime hours, that's where the saving and the safety advice line up.

On Cosy Octopus, the 1pm to 4pm window was 13.59p on 27 November 2026. An e-bike drawing about 125 watts uses about 0.375 kWh in those three hours, so most of a charge fits inside it. Start it at noon and the whole four hours costs about 9p, against 13p on a flat tariff.

A mobility scooter's eight hours won't fit in one window, but finishing by 4pm still helps. Charging from 8am to 4pm on Cosy works out at about 22p, against 26p on a flat tariff, if the charger draws evenly. Real chargers slow down as the battery fills, so treat that as an estimate. What matters more is missing the 4pm to 7pm peak, when the same charge would cost about 42p.

On a flat tariff, none of this matters. Charge whenever suits you during the day.

## How to keep the cost down

1. **Unplug once it's full.** London Fire Brigade says to unplug the charger once it's finished, and leaving it plugged in gains you nothing.
2. **Measure your own.** A monitoring plug tells you whether your battery takes 0.3 kWh or 0.8 kWh, which changes the yearly figure more than the time of day does.
3. **Avoid the peak on a time-of-use tariff.** On Cosy, a charge between 4pm and 7pm costs about three times what it would in the cheap afternoon hours.
4. **Check your own price.** These figures are for Yorkshire. Your rate depends on your region and tariff, and the sum works the same way with your number.
