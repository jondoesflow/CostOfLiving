*Updated 28 October 2026, 1:35pm: three energy measures announced so far. More to follow when the speech ends and the Budget documents are published.*

Short answer first: **three things are announced so far. A £100 payment this winter for around 20 million households in council tax bands A to D, paid straight into bank accounts. VAT-free electricity carries on until July, instead of ending in March. And gas standing charges are being scrapped, from 2027 at the earliest, with the cost moved onto the unit rate. Everything else below is still reporting until the Budget documents are out.**

The Autumn Budget is today, Wednesday 28 October 2026. It is Healey's first as Chancellor. The government hasn't published a start time, but in recent years the Chancellor has stood up around midday, after Prime Minister's Questions, [according to Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/autumn-budget-2026-when-is-it-and-what-will-it-contain-aQqC47O24jnl).

I wrote the original version of this before the speech, so the "what's expected" section further down is reporting, not policy. I've said where each item came from, so you can judge it for yourself. Confirmed measures go in the section straight below, and I'll update it again once the Budget documents are out.

## Confirmed so far

### A £100 energy payment this winter

The Chancellor announced a £100 payment to help with energy costs this winter, [reported by Sky News](https://news.sky.com/share/12593360) and [GB News](https://www.gbnews.com/money/energy-bill-support-budget-2026-john-healey). According to GB News, Healey said it will reach 20 million households in council tax bands A to D, paid directly into bank accounts.

This isn't the Warm Home Discount top-up that was trailed. It's a separate payment, and it goes by council tax band rather than benefits, so far more households get it.

What isn't clear yet: exactly when it's paid, which nations it covers, whether you need to do anything, and how it reaches people who don't pay council tax by direct debit. I'll add those details from the Budget documents. Until the official details are out, be wary of any text or email asking for your bank details to "claim" it. Scammers jump on announcements like this within hours.

### VAT-free electricity extended to July

The VAT cut on household electricity was due to end on 31 March 2027. The Chancellor has extended it "through the winter and spring until July", according to the [Pembrokeshire Herald's live coverage](https://pembrokeshire-herald.com/152908/live-budget-2026-chancellor-john-healey-unveils-tax-energy-and-spending-plans/), which puts it at around £50 off the annual price cap. [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/uk-budget-healey-walks-a-fiscal-tightrope-as-2027-growth-outlook-darkens-202610281314) and Sky News reported the same extension.

You don't need to do anything. It's applied to the price per unit, so it comes off automatically, including on fixed tariffs, as the original cut did. The exact end date will be in the Budget documents.

### Gas standing charges will be scrapped

The Chancellor has confirmed that standing charges will come off gas bills. In his words, as [reported by MoneySavingExpert](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2026/10/energy-standing-charges-scrapped/): "Gas Standing Charges are unfair and outdated so I am scrapping them."

What we know, from MoneySavingExpert's report:

- **When:** "in 2027 or as soon as possible after". MoneySavingExpert expects it to arrive with the October 2027 or January 2028 price cap. It isn't happening this winter.
- **Where:** England, Scotland and Wales, for around 23 million households.
- **Who pays instead:** the cost moves onto the gas unit rate. The standing charge is currently about £108 a year. MoneySavingExpert estimates the average gas unit rate could go from 7.97p to about 9.11p per kWh.
- **Winners and losers:** lower gas users pay less, higher users pay more. Martin Lewis estimates about 60% of households will pay less and 40% more, with the dividing line somewhere around 12,500 kWh of gas a year (his estimate). The government puts the saving at about £45 a year on a typical bill.
- **Electricity standing charges stay for now.** The government says it will set out more next year and work with Ofgem on reducing them.
- **Still unknown:** whether it applies to every tariff and payment type, including prepayment. The government says "targeted mitigation for a small number of households" will follow.

So if you live alone, or in a well-insulated flat, this should help. If you heat a large or draughty house with gas, you may pay a little more. Either way, it doesn't change anything about your bill this winter.

## Where bills stand going into the Budget

It helps to know the starting point, because it explains why energy is near the top of the list.

From 1 October to 31 December 2026, [Ofgem's price cap](https://ofgem.gov.uk/press-release/energy-price-cap-will-rise-4-october-2026) is £1,723 a year for a typical household paying for gas and electricity by Direct Debit. That's up 4%, or £60 a year, on the summer. Ofgem puts the rise down to higher wholesale gas prices from the conflict in the Middle East, with wholesale prices up 11% over the three months it looked at.

The detail matters more than the headline. [Ofgem's figures](https://ofgem.gov.uk/news/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-october-and-31-december-2026) for the default tariff, averaged across Great Britain:

| | Unit rate | Standing charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electricity | 26.32p per kWh | 54.83p a day |
| Gas | 7.97p per kWh | 29.68p a day |

Gas bills went up 8% this time. Electricity stayed broadly flat, because the government took VAT off household electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Ofgem says that without it, the rise would have been about £45 higher.

The bigger worry is January. Cornwall Insight, the consultancy most people quote on the cap, [forecast on 30 September](https://www.cornwall-insight.com/press-releases/16-price-cap-rise-forecast-in-january/) that the January to March 2027 cap could rise 16% to £1,999 a year, an increase of £276. It blamed disrupted gas supplies and low gas storage in Europe. Ofgem confirms the real figure by 25 November.

So the Budget lands just before what could be the biggest jump in bills since January 2023. That's the backdrop.

## What's already changed this year

Two measures from earlier in the year are already in your bill, and both are relevant to today, because the Budget could extend or build on them.

**The £150 cut from April 2026.** The November 2025 Budget [took an average of £150 off household bills](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/what-does-the-autumn-budget-mean-for-your-energy-bills) from April this year. It did it by ending the Energy Company Obligation (an insulation scheme paid for through bills) on 31 March 2026, worth £59, and moving 75% of the Renewables Obligation onto general taxation, worth £88. Lower VAT on both added £7.

**The VAT cut on electricity.** As above, VAT came off household electricity on 1 October. It's worth about £45 a year to a typical household. It was due to end on 31 March 2027, and today's Budget has extended it to July (see "Confirmed so far").

## What's expected today, and who reported it

Here's what has been trailed in the run-up. None of this is confirmed.

### More money for poorer households through the Warm Home Discount

The Warm Home Discount is currently £150 a year, taken off the electricity bill of households on qualifying means-tested benefits such as Pension Credit and Universal Credit. Around six million households got it last year after it was widened.

The Guardian reported that Healey is considering adding another £100 to it, as part of more than £1bn of support for less well-off households, [as relayed by The Independent](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/poorer-uk-households-could-given-081831584.html). [Which? also covered it](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/autumn-budget-2026-when-is-it-and-what-will-it-contain-aQqC47O24jnl), noting this top-up would be paid for by taxpayers rather than bill-payers. That matters, because the existing discount is funded through everyone's bills.

If it happens, this is the measure most likely to make a real difference this winter for the households struggling most.

### Cutting standing charges on gas (now confirmed, see above)

The Telegraph reported that the Prime Minister has asked Healey and the Energy Secretary, Miatta Fahnbulleh, to look at scrapping standing charges on gas bills, [according to LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/john-healey-budget-2026-5HjdkXh_2/). Together with limits on the Energy Secretary's power to add net zero charges to household bills, the report says this could cut household gas bills by up to £50 a year.

Standing charges are the daily fee you pay before you use anything. They've been unpopular for years because they hit low users hardest. On the current cap, the gas one alone is about £108 a year (29.68p a day).

I'd treat this one with some caution. Scrapping a standing charge doesn't make the cost disappear: it has to be recovered somewhere, usually through a higher unit rate. Whether that's good for you depends on how much gas you use.

### Moving more levies off bills

There's an argument going on inside government about the remaining policy costs on bills. [The Independent reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/poorer-uk-households-could-given-081831584.html) that Fahnbulleh has called for all the remaining green levies to come off bills, that Healey has pushed back, and that Fahnbulleh hinted the cost could be shared through taxation.

Consumer groups want the same. Which? has called for the remaining environmental and social charges to be moved off electricity bills and funded from general taxation, and estimates that [could save households £75 to £286 a year](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/autumn-budget-2026-when-is-it-and-what-will-it-contain-aQqC47O24jnl). Bloomberg reported that businesses are [hopeful Healey will cut levies](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/uk-firms-optimistic-healey-will-remove-levies-from-energy-bills) from their electricity bills too.

This is the most expensive option on the table, which is exactly why it's the least certain.

### A freeze on January's fuel duty rise

Not a household energy bill, but it's in the same conversation. Fuel duty is due to rise on 1 January, [from roughly 53p to nearly 56p a litre](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/autumn-budget-2026-when-is-it-and-what-will-it-contain-aQqC47O24jnl), after the temporary cut was unwound. [LBC reports](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/john-healey-budget-2026-5HjdkXh_2/) that Healey is expected to cancel that rise.

## What the Chancellor has said

Healey has been careful not to pre-announce anything, but the direction is clear. He told ITV News that "energy costs are punishing at the moment" and that the government will "do what we can in the Budget" for households and businesses, [as reported by Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/autumn-budget-2026-when-is-it-and-what-will-it-contain-aQqC47O24jnl).

Ahead of the speech, he said he wants "to start the long-term action needed to get energy costs down for good", and that "conflicts abroad are pushing up energy prices", [according to LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/john-healey-budget-2026-5HjdkXh_2/).

He's also sticking with the fiscal rules set by Rachel Reeves: day-to-day spending paid for by taxes rather than borrowing, and debt falling as a share of the economy, both by 2029/30. That's the brake on all of the above. Every pound of bill support has to be found somewhere else.

## What you can do now, whatever the Budget says

The honest truth is that none of these, even if they all happen, would fully offset a 16% rise in January if Cornwall Insight's forecast is right. So it's worth doing the boring things now rather than waiting.

**Look at a fixed tariff.** Ofgem's own Neil Kenward said in August that fixed tariffs were available "at £100 or more below the October price cap". With a big January rise forecast, fixing could be worth it. Check exit fees and compare against what you'd pay on the cap, not just the headline price.

**Check you're getting the Warm Home Discount if you qualify.** [GOV.UK says](https://www.gov.uk/the-warm-home-discount-scheme) you'll usually get it automatically if you're eligible, but in Scotland you may need to apply to your energy supplier. It's taken off your bill rather than paid to you, and prepayment customers can get it as a top-up voucher. If you're on a qualifying benefit and haven't had it before, that page links to the rules for each country.

**Use your own usage to decide.** If you have a smart meter, our [Should I switch? tool](/should-i-switch/) takes your half-hourly usage file and works out what you'd have paid on each tariff, including the time-of-use ones. If you're on Agile, my earlier post on [who Agile suits](/blog/octopus-agile-explained-who-it-suits/) is worth a read before January.

**Cut gas use first.** This time it's gas driving the rise. Turning the boiler's flow temperature down is free and one of the more effective changes. I've written about [how to do it](/blog/boiler-flow-temperature-60-degrees-cut-gas-bill/).
