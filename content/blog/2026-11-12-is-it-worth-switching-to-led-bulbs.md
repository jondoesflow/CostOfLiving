Is it worth switching to LED bulbs? For any light you use most evenings, yes, and it usually pays for itself within a few months. A 60W traditional bulb left on for 5 hours a day costs about £28.82 a year at today's price cap. The LED that gives the same light costs £3.36.

The catch is the lights you hardly use. A bulb in the loft or the spare room might take years to pay back, so start with the ones that are on every night.

Here are the numbers, and how I got them.

## The sum behind every figure here

Running cost is power × time × price. I've used the Ofgem price cap rate for electricity from 1 October to 31 December 2026, which is 26.32p per kWh for the Great Britain average on Direct Debit ([Ofgem's announcement](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/news/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-october-and-31-december-2026)). Your own rate is on your bill.

For the time, I've assumed a light is on for 5 hours a day, every day of the year. That's my assumption, not a measured figure, but it's a fair guess for a kitchen or living room light in winter. Lights you use less save less.

The LED wattages and the old bulbs they replace come from the product pages at Simply LED, checked on 3 November 2026. Simply LED's pages show their own running costs too, but at the October 2024 price cap of 24.50p, so mine are a little higher.

## How quickly an LED pays for itself

This is the bit that decides whether it's worth it. Using the prices listed on 3 November 2026, and still 5 hours a day:

- **A pack of three 7W LEDs at £7.27** replacing three 60W bulbs saves £25.46 a bulb a year, so the pack pays for itself in about 2 months.
- **A single 3.8W "ultra-efficient" LED at £9.07** replacing a 60W bulb saves £26.99 a year, paying back in about 5 months. It costs more up front and saves a little more each year.
- **A pack of ten 4W GU10 spotlights at £6.94** replacing ten 50W halogens saves £221 a year between them. That pack pays for itself in under a month.

Now the other end. For a light that's on for one hour a week, swapping a 60W bulb for an LED saves about 72p a year. A £9.07 bulb would take around 12 years to pay that back. If the old bulb still works and you barely use that light, leave it until it fails.

## Spotlights are where the money hides

The kitchen is the room I'd look at first. A lot of homes have six, eight or ten halogen spotlights on one switch.

Say you have ten 50W GU10 halogens on for 5 hours a day. That's about £240 a year at the price cap. Ten 4W LED spotlights doing the same job cost about £19, a saving of around £221 a year. It's the biggest single lighting saving most homes have, and it's ten bulbs and a stepladder.

## Choosing the right LED

A few things are worth checking before you buy, because a wrong bulb is the most common reason people give up on LEDs:

1. **Match the fitting.** Look at the old bulb's base: B22 is the push-and-twist bayonet, E27 the large screw, E14 the small screw, and GU10 the spotlight with two stubby pins.
2. **Match the brightness by lumens, not watts.** Watts tell you how much power a bulb uses, not how bright it is. Both of the 60W replacements above give 806 lumens, so for a 60W swap look for an LED of around 800.
3. **Check it's dimmable if you have a dimmer switch.** The product page says whether it is.
4. **Pick the colour you like.** All the bulbs above are warm white (3000K), which I'd choose for a living room. Cool white suits a garage or a workshop.

## What about switching lights off?

Turning a light off when you leave a room always saves something, LED or not. With an LED, though, the amounts get small. A 7W LED costs about 1p for a whole 8-hour night. Swapping the bulb does far more than nagging anyone to switch it off. The same logic applies to gadgets on standby, which I worked through in [which gadgets cost the most on standby](/blog/standby-power-cost-uk-switch-off-at-the-wall/).

## The short version

- A 60W bulb on for 5 hours a day costs about £28.82 a year at the price cap; its LED replacement costs about £3.36.
- Most swaps pay for themselves within a few months on a light you use every evening.
- Halogen spotlights in the kitchen are usually the biggest saving in the house.
- Leave rarely used lights until the old bulb fails.
