# [Your town] developer builds free tool to cut the cost of running household appliances

**A free website, Cheap Watts, tells households the cheapest time to run their washing machine, dishwasher or car charger on their own electricity tariff, and what it will cost.**

[Your town], [date]: A [your town] developer has launched Cheap Watts (cheapwatts.co.uk), a free website that helps UK households cut their electricity bills by working out when, and how, to use their appliances.

People pick their supplier and tariff, then an appliance, and the site shows the cheapest time to run it, what it will cost then compared with right now, and what to set the appliance's delay timer to. It uses live prices from Octopus Energy, EDF and E.ON Next, and anyone with another supplier can enter their own rates.

The difference can be bigger than people expect. On one evening in October 2026, live Agile Octopus prices in Yorkshire meant the same washing machine cycle cost 31p if started at teatime, or 13p if started later that night.

The site is also honest when timing doesn't matter. Most households are on standard tariffs, where electricity costs the same at any time of day, and Cheap Watts says so rather than suggesting savings that aren't there.

Alongside the main tool, Cheap Watts includes:

- **Plan my evening:** fits several appliances into the cheapest hours, one at a time if needed.
- **Gas or electric?:** compares everyday jobs at today's prices, such as the kettle against the hob, or a heated airer against a tumble dryer.
- **Solar and batteries:** shows whether to use or sell solar power, and when to charge a home battery.
- **Should I switch?:** estimates what a household would have paid on each tariff, using their own smart meter data, which never leaves their device.

The site is free, with no sign-up and no adverts (a few results link to related products, clearly labelled), works on any phone, and is built to the WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standard.

**Notes to editors**

- Cheap Watts is free to use and is not affiliated with any energy supplier.
- Prices come from the public price data published by Octopus Energy, EDF and E.ON Next. Carbon figures come from National Grid's regional carbon intensity forecast.
- The 31p and 13p example uses live Agile Octopus prices for the Yorkshire region on 22 October 2026. Agile prices change every half hour, so figures on other days will differ.
- Fire services advise against leaving washing machines, tumble dryers and dishwashers running overnight or while out. Cheap Watts links to that advice in its guides; some tariffs also have cheap windows in the evening or afternoon.
